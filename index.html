📊 Metrics Explanation

  • Success Rate (%): Measures the fraction of examples for which the model returns a parsable numeric prediction. Higher is better.
  • Hit@X%: The percentage of predictions within X% relative tolerance of the ground truth. For example, Hit@90% means the prediction is within 90% accuracy (\(\tau = 0.10\)). Calculated as: \(\frac{|\hat{y}_i - y_i|}{\max(1, y_i)} \leq \tau\). Higher is better.
  • MAE (Mean Absolute Error): The average absolute difference between predicted counts and ground truth. Formula: \(\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} |\hat{y}_i - y_i|\). Lower is better.
  • MSE (Mean Squared Error): The average squared differences between predictions and ground truth, which penalizes larger errors more heavily. Formula: \(\text{MSE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (\hat{y}_i - y_i)^2\). Lower is better.
  • Per-difficulty: Metrics are reported for Easy, Medium, and Hard difficulty levels.
  • Per-capability: Metrics are reported for Pattern, Reasoning, and Semantic capabilities.
